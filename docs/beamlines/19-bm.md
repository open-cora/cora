# 19-BM

*Micro-CT at the Advanced Photon Source, in commissioning, and the beamline
whose survey was the most useful of the four despite returning almost
nothing. Nothing is deployed there; what exists is a one-row descriptor and
the reason it has one row.*

A beamline descriptor is what a running keeper has to be told about the
beamline it serves. The rule that keeps it short is in
[`beamlines/README.md`](https://github.com/open-cora/cora/blob/main/beamlines/README.md).

## The device register

[`beamlines/19-bm/devices.toml`](https://github.com/open-cora/cora/blob/main/beamlines/19-bm/devices.toml)
holds one device, a sample rotation stage, confirmed.

It holds one because one is what exists. Asked which records it drives, the
acquisition software named a rotation stage and two sample axes. The
rotation stage is real and was reading 22.4 degrees when it was asked. The
two sample axes came back as `TODO_SAMPLE_X` and `TODO_SAMPLE_Y`. The
beamline is in commissioning and nobody has filled them in.

## What that broke, and it was one of our own rules

**A reference here is checked for shape and cannot be checked for
existence.** Neither placeholder contains a `.` or a trailing `:`, so both
pass normalization unchanged, and the loader accepts either as a device. A
register holding `TODO_SAMPLE_X` would be well formed. It would carry
`confirmed = true` with exactly as much justification as every other row in
this directory, because it came from the same beamline by the same
technique. And it would name nothing at all.

**So asking the acquisition software what it drives yields a candidate and
not a device.** That was already known to miss things: at 32-ID it named
three of five motors and the other two had to be found another way. Missing
a row is visible. Inventing one is not, and this beamline is where that
turned up.

The technique now has a third step. Every answer is read back with a second
`caget` before it becomes a row, which costs one call per device and is the
only thing standing between a register and a confident, well-formed lie.
That is written into the rule rather than left as a habit.

## The one row does not pick out one device

Two servers claim this beamline's record names. Every motor from `m17` upward
answers from both a link-local address and the beamline's own soft IOC host,
and the rotation stage in the register is one of them. Channel Access reports
the collision and picks one, and the one it picks is the link-local address,
an interface that came up unconfigured rather than a machine anybody meant to
serve these records.

**That is a fault at the beamline, not a gap in the descriptor.** It is worth
stating here because the register cannot express it and should not learn to.
A row says where a device is published and what this system calls it; it has
no way to say that two things answer. Equipment can already say the useful
part, which is that a device is `Faulted`, and that is the honest status for
one whose address does not identify it.

It also touches the assumption underneath the whole scheme. A record name was
chosen as the identity because it is the only string independent clients must
agree on. The loader guards one direction, two spellings of one motor becoming
two rows. This is the other direction, one name serving two devices, and
nothing here catches it or could.

## What is documented and not registered

The facility's own pages describe slits and filters on this beamline's motor
IOC. The acquisition software does not name them, and this survey did not
guess at their addresses from a record number, so they are absent rather
than unconfirmed. The detector is named as a prefix, which is a namespace
and not a record, the same exclusion every register here makes.

**19-BM starts no control software at boot**, so an idle beamline and an
unreachable one look identical from outside. That was true when this survey
was planned and is worth keeping in mind before reading an empty result as a
network problem. It was running when the survey was taken.
