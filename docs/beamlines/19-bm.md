# 19-BM

*Micro-CT at the Advanced Photon Source, in commissioning. A conductor and a
reporter run here against a simulator, and the whole chain from dispatch to a
described file has been walked. Its survey was the most instructive of the
four, and what made it so was two rows the register does not have.*

A beamline descriptor is what a running keeper has to be told about the
beamline it serves. The rule that keeps it short is in
[`beamlines/README.md`](https://github.com/open-cora/cora/blob/main/beamlines/README.md).

## The device register

[`beamlines/19-bm/devices.toml`](https://github.com/open-cora/cora/blob/main/beamlines/19-bm/devices.toml)
holds sixteen devices in six groups, all confirmed. It is the largest
register in the directory.

| Group | Devices |
| --- | --- |
| `sample-stack` | sample rotation, hexapod base Y |
| `white-beam-slits` | outboard, top, inboard, bottom |
| `filters` | upstream, downstream |
| `target` | X, Y |
| `optics` | X, Y, high-magnification focus and rotation |
| `tomo-centering` | at 0 degrees, at 90 degrees |

**The rows come from two sources, and only one of them is strong.** The
rotation stage was named by the acquisition software, which answers with a
role rather than a crate position and is record-to-record with nothing
translated in between. The other fifteen come from reading every motor on
the IOC for its description field and its readback, and a row exists only
where both answered. A description field is served empty and writable by
anyone, so what it gives is a hint at a role rather than a reference. Each
name here is authored in the register from that hint, not copied out of it.

## The two rows that are not there

Asked which records it drives, the acquisition software named a rotation
stage and two sample axes. The rotation stage is real and was reading 22.4
degrees when it was asked. The two sample axes came back as `TODO_SAMPLE_X`
and `TODO_SAMPLE_Y`. The beamline is in commissioning and nobody has filled
them in.

**A reference here is checked for shape and cannot be checked for
existence.** Neither placeholder contains a `.` or a trailing `:`, so both
pass normalization unchanged, and the loader accepts either as a device. A
register holding `TODO_SAMPLE_X` would be well formed. It would carry
`confirmed = true` with exactly as much justification as every other row in
this directory, because it came from the same beamline by the same
technique. And it would name nothing at all.

**So asking the acquisition software what it drives yields a candidate and
not a device.** That was already known to miss things: at 32-ID it named
three of five motors and the other two had to be found another way. Missing
a row is visible. Inventing one is not, and this beamline is where that
turned up.

The technique now has a third step. Every answer is read back with a second
`caget` before it becomes a row, which costs one call per device and is the
only thing standing between a register and a confident, well-formed lie.
That is written into the rule rather than left as a habit.

## Nine motors answered and are deliberately absent

`m7` and `m8` describe themselves as spare. `m12` through `m16`, `m19` and
`m20` describe themselves by crate and channel, which says where a cable
lands and not what it moves. Every one of them reads zero and belongs to
nothing anybody has named.

They are left out rather than written down without a group, because a row
whose name would have to be invented is the guessing this directory exists
to avoid. The register records that the sweep saw them and decided, so a
later reader does not have to wonder whether it missed them.

## A row picked out no device when this was written, and picks one out now

This section used to say that two servers claimed this beamline's record
names: that every reference in the register drew a duplicate-server warning
from Channel Access, and that every one answered from a link-local address
rather than from the beamline's own soft IOC host.

Re-measured from orco on 2026-10-03, and none of it reproduces. One server
answers, it is `hounsfield.xray.aps.anl.gov` at `10.54.129.24`, it is the
beamline's own soft IOC host, and it serves on the standard port. No warning
appears on the facility broadcast list, nor against that single address
pinned:

```
   19bmSoft:aero:m1   hounsfield.xray.aps.anl.gov:5064   22.4088949931
   19bmSoft:m1        hounsfield.xray.aps.anl.gov:5064   9.75
   19bmSoft:m2        hounsfield.xray.aps.anl.gov:5064   2.7004
   19bmSoft:m23       hounsfield.xray.aps.anl.gov:5064   0.0
```

The earlier reading is recorded rather than deleted, because a race between
two servers comes and goes by nature. One measurement showing it absent is
weaker evidence than one showing it present, so a reader who meets it again
should know it has been seen here before.

**What changed is what the register's `confirmed = true` claims.** It always
meant that something answered and gave a role. While two servers were
answering, that was all it could mean; it now also means the name picks out
one device, which is the half that matters to anything writing.

It is still worth stating why the register does not express the ambiguity and
should not learn to. A row says where a device is published and what this
system calls it, and has no way to say that two things answer. Equipment can
already say the useful part, which is that a device is `Faulted`, and that is
the honest status for one whose address does not identify it.

The assumption underneath the scheme is unchanged either way. A record name is
the identity because it is the only string independent clients must agree on.
The loader guards one direction, two spellings of one motor becoming two rows.
The other direction, one name serving two devices, is caught by nothing here
and could not be.

**What this removes is one of the two things standing between this beamline
and a conductor.** Sixteen confirmed rows now describe 19-BM and name devices
a writer would reach deterministically. What remains is on the host rather
than in the register: `FACTUSER`'s `epics.env` pins that same address to port
`16661` where the server listens on `5064`, so a client reading that file
finds nothing while the broadcast list works.

## What is documented and not registered

The detector is named as a prefix, ending in a namespace separator and naming
no record, which is the same exclusion every other register here makes.

**19-BM starts no control software at boot**, so an idle beamline and an
unreachable one look identical from outside. That was true when this survey
was planned and is worth keeping in mind before reading an empty result as a
network problem. It was running when the survey was taken.
